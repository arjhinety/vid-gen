What Experimental Machines is for
Serious technical machine-learning research, and widening who can take part in it,
especially researchers based in the Philippines. It is not a consultancy, automation
agency, policy group, incubator, professional association, or awareness campaign.
Proposed purpose language (GOVERNANCE RECOMMENDATION, for Articles
drafting)
The BIR's organizational test looks for a Sec. 30 purpose in the Articles. RMO 38-2019
defines "scientific" as "undertaking or assisting in pure or basic, applied and scientific
research in the field of ... industry, engineering ... physical and natural sciences for the
public interest." Machine learning is not named, so the clause should use those words and
then name the field.
Primary purpose (plain-English sketch, not final wording):
To undertake and assist pure, basic and applied scientific research in machine
learning and related fields of computing, engineering, mathematics and the physical
sciences, for the public interest, and to make the methods, data, code and results of
that research openly available.
Secondary purposes, each "in furtherance of the primary purpose":
1. Publishing and disseminating research, including negative results.
2. Developing and releasing open-source research software, models, datasets and
benchmarks.
3. Training researchers through fellowships, mentorship, workshops and scientific
meetings.
4. Collaborating with universities, research institutes and other non-profit bodies.
5. Receiving grants, donations, compute and equipment, and administering funded
research.

6. Acquiring and holding property needed for research.
Research areas (model training, post-training, optimization, inference, speculative
decoding, quantization, distributed training, architectures, mixture-of-experts,
evaluation, ML systems, hardware-aware and efficient ML, datasets, reproducibility,
methodology, open models and infrastructure) belong in an illustrative "including but not
limited to" list, or better, only in the Mission Statement. Listing them exhaustively in the
Articles would force an amendment every time the research moves.
Wording to avoid in the Articles: "frontier" (undefined), "democratization" (vague),
"benchmarking services" or "consulting" (reads as commercial), and "education" as a
stand-alone purpose. Education is not a Sec. 30(E) purpose; Sec. 30(H) covers schools.
Keep it as a means of the scientific purpose.
Name. This draft uses Experimental Machines as the corporate name. Whether
"Experimental Intelligence" continues as a program or brand name is a FOUNDER
DECISION (see Founder Review Required).
2. Researcher-first design: two layers
Two layers share the same people but answer different questions. The corporate layer
answers "is this lawful, funded and safe for the institution?" The research layer answers
"is this true, and how do we find out?" Neither layer outranks the other in its own domain
(Section 6).

4. Member powers
Members hold the powers that change what Experimental Machines is; the Board runs
it. With three members, "2/3 of all members" means two of three, so thresholds alone
cannot stop two founders acting together. The protections below therefore rely on
process (notice, delay, reasons, an outside reviewer) as much as on vote counts.
"All members" means all voting members on the register, not only those present. Where
the law sets a minimum, the By-Laws may require more but not less (CONSERVATIVE
WORKING ASSUMPTION; counsel to confirm that higher member thresholds in the By-
Laws are enforceable for each item).

Mission-protection rule (GOVERNANCE RECOMMENDATION)
A change to the primary purpose, or to the research-independence section of the By-
Laws, requires all of the following:
1. A written proposal stating the new text and the reasons, circulated to all members at

7. Membership: admission and removal
Membership is a legal governance status that must be granted on purpose; it never
follows automatically from doing research here. The law leaves the rules to you:
membership and its rights are defined by the Articles and By-Laws (RCC Secs. 88–90),
membership is personal and non-transferable unless they say otherwise (Sec. 89), and it
ends "in the manner and for the causes provided in the articles of incorporation or the
bylaws" (Sec. 90). The Corporate Secretary must keep a current list of members (Sec. 92).
Who is not a member (GOVERNANCE RECOMMENDATION)
Researchers, research engineers, employees, fellows, contributors, collaborators, open-
source contributors, event participants, donors and sponsors are not members unless
formally admitted. Contributor, fellowship and employment agreements should say so in
one sentence.
Admission (GOVERNANCE RECOMMENDATION)
Eligibility, at least one of:
sustained research contribution over roughly 12 months or more;
sustained research-engineering contribution;
scientific leadership of an Experimental Machines program;
long-term institutional service;
governance contribution.
Also required: written acceptance of the Articles, By-Laws, Research Independence
Charter and Conflict-of-Interest Policy; disclosure of interests; and no disqualification
under RCC Sec. 26 if the person may later serve as trustee.
Process:
1. Any member proposes a candidate in writing, with reasons tied to the criteria.
2. The Board reviews and nominates (majority of the Board).
3. Members approve by 2/3 of all members.
4. The Secretary enters the new member on the register; membership starts on the date
of entry.
Consequence: with three members, admission needs two of three. That is selective
without letting one founder block every newcomer.

How the three-person board handles a conflict
With one trustee recused, two remain: exactly a quorum. Both must vote yes. If they
disagree, the matter fails. For an insider contract, failing is the safe outcome. If both of the
others are also conflicted (for example, a transaction with a company all three founders
own), the Board cannot approve it and it goes to members for 2/3 ratification with full
disclosure and a fair-and-reasonable showing (RCC Sec. 31). GOVERNANCE
RECOMMENDATION: treat such transactions as presumptively not to be done until the
Board has at least two trustees with no interest in them.
