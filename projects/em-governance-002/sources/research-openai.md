# OpenAI: how the nonprofit and for-profit entities were structured (2015 to 2026-09-29)

Research for the em-governance-002 explainer. Audience: three founders of a small Philippine non-profit ML research lab weighing "the OpenAI move". This file sticks to facts. It gives no opinions and does not discuss Philippine law.

- Method: every URL marked VERIFIED was opened and read in the user's real Chrome browser (Claude in Chrome) on 2026-09-29. Web search was only used to find URLs.
- Labels: **VERIFIED** means I read the fact on the cited page. **UNVERIFIED** means I could not confirm it from a primary source I read; the reason is given. **PRESS** means the fact comes from a news article I read in Chrome, not from a primary document.
- Quotes: exact anchor phrases are kept short. Numbers and dates were copied from the page.
- Arithmetic I did myself is marked "(my arithmetic)".

### § DE conditions: what the Delaware AG required (2025-10-28), VERIFIED
Source: https://news.delaware.gov/2025/10/28/ag-jennings-completes-review-of-openai-recapitalization/

The AG named three goals: (1) the safety mission stays primary, (2) the nonprofit keeps control of the PBC, and (3) the nonprofit is "treated fairly... both financially and in terms of access to OpenAI's technology". The commitments she secured:

1. The nonprofit has the sole power to appoint PBC directors and the power to remove them.
2. The PBC's mission is identical to the nonprofit's and uses the OpenAI Charter.
3. On safety and security, PBC directors may consider only the mission, not stockholders' financial interests.
4. The SSC stays at the nonprofit. It can require mitigations, "up to and including halting the release of models".
5. The SSC chair (Zico Kolter) sits on the nonprofit board only, with full observation rights at the PBC.
6. The nonprofit gets access to the PBC's research, IP, models, APIs, staff and support.
7. Within one year, at least two nonprofit directors (including the SSC chair) are not on the PBC board.
8. The AG gets advance notice of significant governance changes.

### § CA MOU: key terms (signed 2025-10-27), VERIFIED on pages read
- Item 2: control of the PBC lasts "so long as the NFP holds Class N Common Stock". The NFP board alone appoints and removes PBC directors, and decides whether to give up the Class N stock.
- Item 3: the PBC mission is identical to the nonprofit's.
- Item 5: the nonprofit board owes fiduciary duties to the nonprofit, the mission and its public beneficiaries.
- Item 6: the nonprofit holds charitable assets in California.
- Item 7: while it holds Class N stock, the nonprofit's prior written approval is needed for:
  - charter or mission amendments,
  - a sale of material assets or a "Deemed Liquidation Event",
  - issuing more Class N stock or new director-voting stock.
- Item 13: the PBC must provide the nonprofit reasonable services and resources "without compensation".
- Items 14–17: at least one more Foundation-only director within a year, a majority-independent PBC board, independent committees, and annual public reports.
- Item 19: at least **21 days' prior written notice** to the AG before any change of control, change of mission, removal of Class N rights, or HQ move out of California.
- Item 20: the NFP reimburses the AG's review costs.
- Item 21: annual fiduciary-duty training for dual-board members.
- Item 22: the AG will not object, based on the recap being "fair or reasonable from a financial point of view to the NFP".

## (b) Lessons for a small nonprofit, stated as sourced facts

1. **Pledges are not cash.** At launch, funders "committed $1 billion" (2015 post). OpenAI later reported that actual cash donations were **$137M** (2024-12-27 post). OpenAI said it needed far more capital than donations could provide, and that was its stated reason for creating a for-profit.
2. **Control comes from the legal documents.** In 2019, investor and employee paperwork put the Charter ahead of returns, and "the company is controlled by OpenAI Nonprofit's board" (OpenAI LP post). In 2025, control depended on the nonprofit holding **Class N Common Stock** and having the sole power to appoint and remove PBC directors (CA MOU items 2 and 7).
3. **Conflict-of-interest rules were written in from the start.** In 2019, only a minority of board members could hold financial stakes, and only those without stakes could vote where investor and mission interests might conflict (OpenAI LP post). In 2023, OpenAI stated that its independent directors held no equity (2023-11-17 post). In 2024 the rules were tightened: a stronger conflict-of-interest policy and a whistleblower hotline (2024-03-08 post).
4. **The nonprofit board's power to fire the CEO is real, and so is the fallout.** On 2023-11-17 the 501(c)(3) board removed the CEO. Four days later it decided to rehire him, and the board was reconstituted. The later independent review found the board acted within its discretion. It also found the board acted on an abridged timeframe, "without advance notice to key stakeholders" (2023-11-17, 2023-11-29 and 2024-03-08 posts).
5. **Changing a charity's structure brings regulators in, and it takes time.** The Delaware AG's review ran from 2024-10-09 to 2025-10-28. California's ran about 18 months. Delaware hired its own counsel and a financial advisor (Moelis). Under the California MOU, the nonprofit reimburses the AG's review costs (DE release; CA statement; MOU item 20).
6. **Regulators can reshape the plan.** Under the Dec 2024 plan, the PBC would "run and control" operations. By May 2025, after AG dialogue, the nonprofit kept control. The final deal added conditions: the nonprofit appoints and removes directors, the mission stays identical, safety oversight stays at the nonprofit, and the AGs get advance notice of key changes (2024-12-27 post; 2025-05-05 post; DE release; CA MOU).
7. **The nonprofit must receive fair value, checked by independent advisors.** California's non-objection rested on the deal being fair "from a financial point of view to the NFP". The result was a 26% stake worth about $130B, plus a warrant, for the Foundation (CA MOU item 22; our-structure page).
8. **A founder or donor dispute can last years, and courts may decide it on procedure.** Musk sued in Aug 2024, and trial ran from Apr to May 2026. The jury decided his charitable-trust and unjust-enrichment claims on **statute of limitations** grounds, not on whether the restructuring was proper. The judge adopted that verdict. As of 2026-09-29, counterclaims and other matters were still on the docket (ECF 572, 580, 602, 609).

## Where sources disagree or measure different things

- **Musk's contribution.** Press (Reuters via Cyprus Mail) reports Musk's claim that he gave **$38 million**. OpenAI says total cash donations were $137M, "less than a third" from Elon, which puts the ceiling near $45M (my arithmetic). These measure different things and are not necessarily in conflict.
- **Who wanted a for-profit.** OpenAI says Musk wanted a for-profit in 2017 and demanded control. Musk alleged he was manipulated and that OpenAI was "stealing a charity" (PRESS). The jury did **not** decide this question; it ruled on timeliness only.
- **General partner in 2019.** The 2019 post names OpenAI Inc (the nonprofit) as general partner. A separate entity, "OpenAI GP, L.L.C.", exists (docket). The commonly cited diagram, in which the nonprofit controls OpenAI GP LLC and GP LLC controls the capped-profit, could not be verified because the archived page would not load.
- **For-profit entity name.** The 2019 post calls it "OpenAI LP". Microsoft's 10-Q and the Delaware amicus refer to **OpenAI Global, LLC**, and the CA MOU calls it the "for-profit limited liability company". The date of the LP-to-LLC change is UNVERIFIED.
- **When Altman was reinstated.** The board decided on 2023-11-21 (March 2024 post). The formal announcement and new board came on 2023-11-29.
- **Microsoft's stake.** The 32.5% figure excludes OpenAI's recent funding rounds. The 27% figure is as-converted, fully diluted. Both appear in the same OpenAI post.
- **Microsoft money.** The 10-Q reports **$13B committed / $11.6B funded** (equity funding). Press reports that a Microsoft executive testified Microsoft "has spent more than $100 billion on its partnership" (PRESS). This is a different and broader measure. The 2023 "$10B" figure is press only.
- **S-1 date.** OpenAI's post (2026-06-08) says only "recently". One press item says it was submitted around May 22. The same item says the filing "landed two days after" the verdict, which does not fit the verdict date of May 18.
